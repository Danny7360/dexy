# Appendix F: Competitive Landscape

This appendix organizes the market in the style most useful for a Silicon Valley-style crypto startup pitch: not just “who looks similar,” but “what users would otherwise use instead.”

## 1. Competitive Categories

Dexy competes with four categories:

1. native platform tools
2. onchain analytics products
3. trading workflow products
4. user self-assembly

## 2. Native Platform Tools

### Hyperliquid native interface

#### Strengths

- direct execution
- native account visibility
- no extra tool required
- trusted as the default surface

#### Weaknesses

- general-purpose, not operator-specific
- less likely to prioritize deep attribution
- less likely to prioritize wallet-aware explanation as a standalone workflow

#### Why users still leave room for third parties

Because many users still need:

- better alerting
- better prioritization
- richer attribution
- less fragmented workflows

## 3. Onchain Analytics Products

### HyperTracker / CoinMarketMan

#### What they do well

- wallet dashboards
- Hyperliquid intelligence
- premium data packaging
- trader-friendly surfaces

#### Why they matter

They prove there is a paid category here.

#### Why Dexy still has room

Dexy can be narrower and more workflow-specific:

- risk and attribution first
- alert first
- one-line action priority first

### Nansen

#### What it does well

- wallet analytics
- smart-money framing
- broad onchain intelligence

#### Why it matters

Nansen proves that public onchain data can still power a real subscription business.

#### Why Dexy still has room

Dexy is not trying to be a broad, cross-chain intelligence platform.

It is trying to own a narrower Hyperliquid operator wedge.

### CoinGlass

#### What it does well

- liquidation maps
- funding data
- broad market statistics

#### Why it matters

CoinGlass proves users will repeatedly consume derivatives risk data.

#### Why Dexy still has room

Dexy is wallet-aware and user-specific, not just market-level.

## 4. Trading Workflow Products

### 3Commas

#### What it does well

- workflow
- automation
- repeat operational use

#### Why it matters

3Commas shows that workflow can be more monetizable than pure analytics.

#### Why Dexy still has room

Dexy starts earlier in the stack:

- interpretation
- risk ranking
- attribution

before execution automation.

### Hyperdash

#### What it does well

- terminal
- copy / counter trading
- fee-on-flow monetization

#### Why it matters

Hyperdash shows how a product can graduate from interface tooling into revenue participation through trading flow.

#### Why Dexy still has room

Dexy can begin with a narrower operator-control wedge without inheriting all the support, compliance, or execution complexity of a full trading surface.

### trade[XYZ]

#### What it does well

- Hyperliquid-native builder surface
- fee participation
- new market exposure

#### Why it matters

trade[XYZ] demonstrates the long-term upside of workflow touching actual trade flow.

#### Why Dexy still has room

Dexy does not need to start there.

It can validate a smaller wedge first.

## 5. The Hidden Competitor: User Self-Assembly

This is arguably the most dangerous competitor.

The alternative to Dexy is often not “another startup.”

It is:

- native UI
- one or two dashboards
- spreadsheets
- Telegram alerts
- manual interpretation

If Dexy cannot beat this messy stack in convenience and clarity, it will not win.

## 6. Direct vs Adjacent vs Substitute

### Direct competitors

- Hyperliquid-native analytics dashboards
- wallet dashboards
- premium alerting surfaces

### Adjacent competitors

- Nansen
- CoinGlass
- Arkham
- generic portfolio or wallet intelligence products

### Substitute workflows

- self-built scripts
- manual wallet review
- group chats and Telegram feeds
- native platform plus exported data

## 7. Where Dexy Should Position

Dexy should not position against the market as:

- “another dashboard”
- “another analytics tool”

Dexy should position as:

- the operator control layer
- the place where risk becomes priority
- the place where loss becomes explanation

## 8. Strategic White Space

The white space Dexy should target is:

- Hyperliquid-first
- wallet-specific
- attribution-first
- alert-first
- action-priority-native

That is narrower than most analytics products and lighter than full execution products.

## 9. Competitive Risks

### Risk 1: native catch-up

Hyperliquid may improve its own tooling.

### Risk 2: analytics commoditization

AI and public data will continue to reduce the cost of basic wallet analysis.

### Risk 3: workflow competition

Execution products may move “upstream” into attribution and risk.

## 10. Strategic Response

Dexy’s best defense is not broader feature expansion.

It is tighter workflow ownership:

- wallet-specific alerts
- stronger explanation quality
- better priority ordering
- repeated operator habit
